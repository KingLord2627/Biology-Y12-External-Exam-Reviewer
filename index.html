📋 Lincoln Index: 8 Mandatory Steps

  1. Determine focus organism: Set trap for organisms in suitable habitat.
  2. Time duration: When identified time has passed, collect trapped specimens.
  3. Mark specimens: Count trapped organisms, mark with appropriate method (tagging, marks on shell, dusting with colour).
  4. Release: Release marked organisms back into habitat. Wait for identified dispersion time to pass.
  5. Reset trap: Set trap again in identical habitat conditions.
  6. Second collection: When identified time has passed, collect trapped specimens.
  7. Count specimens: Count total trapped ($n$) and count marked specimens in trap ($m$).
  8. Estimate: Use Lincoln index formula $N = \frac{M \times n}{m}$.
⚠️ Critical QCAA Exam Rule: Only relies on living organisms! If a marked organism dies, $m$ decreases, skewing data to overrepresent population size.

⚠️ Bradshaw Overview Page 7 Anomaly Detected!

You have only 2 individuals total across 2 species ($N=2, s=2$). Mathematically, $\sum n(n-1) = 0$, producing $SDI = 1 - (0/2) = 1.0$. This is biologically erroneous: the habitat has near-zero biodiversity in reality, but the index registers maximum diversity due to microscopic sample size!

Growth Curves: J-Curve vs S-Curve

Exponential (r-selected) vs Logistic (K-selected) Growth

Exponential Growth (J-Curve)

No limiting factors. Common in $r$-selected species (e.g. bacteria, weeds) until sudden resource collapse.

Logistic Growth (S-Curve)

Growth rate decelerates as density increases, plateauing at carrying capacity ($K$). Common in $K$-selected species.

QCAA Biology Unit 3 Topic 1 Formula Master Sheet

Lincoln Index (Capture-Recapture)
N = (M × n) / m
  • N: Estimated population size
  • M: Number marked in 1st capture
  • n: Total caught in 2nd capture
  • m: Number marked in 2nd capture
Simpson's Diversity Index (SDI)
SDI = 1 - [ Σ n(n - 1) / N(N - 1) ]
  • n: Organisms of a single species
  • N: Total organisms of all species
  • Σ: Sum of values
  • Values close to 1 = high diversity
Population Growth Rate (r)
r = (b + i) - (d + e)
  • b: Births / hatched
  • i: Immigrated in
  • d: Deaths
  • e: Emigrated out
Species Richness (Menhinick Index)
S = s / √N
  • S: Richness index value
  • s: Number of unique species
  • N: Total organisms sampled
Relative Abundance (Evenness)
RA = (TS / TP) × 100
  • RA: Relative abundance (%)
  • TS: Total organisms for 1 species
  • TP: Total organisms in ecosystem
Percentage Frequency
PF = (Sites Present / Total Sites) × 100
  • Assesses how widely dispersed a species is
  • Commonly measured with quadrats
Ecosystem Classification

Specht's Classification System

Classifies Australian plant communities based on two distinct structural criteria:

1. Growth Form of Tallest Stratum:

Trees > 30m, Trees 10–30m, Trees < 10m, Shrubs > 2m, Shrubs < 2m, Hummock grasses, Tussock grasses, Herbs.

2. Foliage Projective Cover (% of ground shaded):

• Dense (70–100%)
• Mid-dense (30–70%)
• Sparse (10–30%)
• Very sparse (< 10%)

Example: Tall trees (10-30m) with 30-70% cover = Open Forest.

Global Bioclimatic System

Holdridge Life Zone Scheme

A global bioclimatic classification scheme based on three empirical climatic axes:

1. Annual Precipitation:

Total yearly rainfall (in mm, logarithmic scale).

2. Mean Annual Biotemperature:

Mean of unit temperatures between 0°C and 30°C (temperatures below 0°C count as 0°C as growth ceases).

3. Potential Evapotranspiration Ratio (PET):

Ratio of annual potential evapotranspiration to mean annual precipitation.

Comparison: r-Strategists vs K-Strategists

Trait / Characteristic r-Strategists (Opportunistic) K-Strategists (Equilibrium)
Lifespan Short lifespans (typically) Long lifespans
Body Size Smaller in size Larger sizes (generally)
Maturation & Reproduction Age Fast maturation; early reproductive ages Slow maturation; later reproductive ages
Number of Offspring Many offspring per clutch/litter Few offspring at a time
Parental Investment & Care Low levels of care for offspring High levels of parental care
Competition Ability Not competitive for resources Highly competitive for resources
Typical Environment Unstable, pioneer, disturbed ecosystems Stable, climax ecosystems at carrying capacity
Growth Curve J-Curve (Exponential growth) S-Curve (Logistic growth to K)
